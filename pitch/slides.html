A new bookkeeper's first cleanup happens on a paying client's books

Who

A new US freelance bookkeeper, 25–45, who has finished a course or the free QuickBooks certification.

When

The first cleanup job: months of messy books, bank-feed duplicates, entries typed in by hand.

What goes wrong

They force the reconciliation to $0.00 by deleting the wrong thing, or leave owner money booked as income. The client's profit figure is wrong.

What we know, what we infer, and what we don't

Observed
  • Newly certified bookkeepers ask how to get hands-on experience (Intuit community, 2023 and 2024).
  • A paid product (QBO Gym, now in Akadian) sells "hands-on experience before taking on real clients".
  • Personal training spend on offer: ProAdvisor free; Intuit cert on Coursera $59/mo; Akadian bundle $847; Bookkeeper Launch $2,999.
  • Cleanup is sold as freelance work on Upwork and Fiverr.
Inferred
  • New freelancers already pay out of pocket to become client-ready.
  • The gap between a certificate and a first paid cleanup is real, and someone already charges to fill it.
  • Recurring forum errors (duplicates, transfers booked as income, deleted reconciled items) are teachable patterns.
Unknown
  • Willingness to pay for this simulator.
  • Retention after the first cases.
  • Buyer age mix; competitor sales volumes.
  • Our evidence base is small: forum posts and price pages. Reddit could not be fetched.
Prices are list offers, not verified sales. Sources in PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md in the repo.3

Free practice exists. Feedback on your actual books doesn't.

QuickBooks Online Test DriveFree sample company. Nothing tells you when you're wrong.
Courses and certificationTeach the software and the theory, not a messy client file.
ChatGPT, YouTubeExplain what to do. Can't see what you actually did to the books.
Paid practice (Akadian / QBO Gym)Closest competitor. Its format and grading we could not verify.

Our angle: one realistic client file at a time, graded on the end state, catching "balanced but wrong", ending with a summary you'd send the client. An advantage we have not yet proven with users.

Every file is proven solvable before you see it

2 of 524,288

Case 2 states tried by the test suite: every combination of deletions, ticks and recording. Only the two valid solutions pass; many others reach $0.00 and still fail, by design.

  • A truth model per transaction, four independent checks, money in integer cents.
  • The generator only returns a file that passes the validator, fails as handed over, and is solved by its own reference solution. 900 generated files are checked in tests.
  • Any correct end state passes; every shortcut (plugging an amount, deleting the uncleared check) has a test.
  • 68 unit tests, a 39-step browser test on the live URL, CI before every deploy. No backend; nothing leaves the browser.
Open source: src/core/generator.ts, tests/, scripts/e2e.mjs7

Who pays, and why they would

Buyer

The new freelance bookkeeper, paying personally, in the months before and after a first cleanup client.

Price we will test: $12 a month

Free: both cases, the file of the day, the skill record. Paid: unlimited weak-spot files and month-end tracks (credit cards, payroll, sales tax). Well under the $59 a month they already pay for courses.

Why it can recur

Practice is a habit, not a purchase: the daily file and the weak-spot loop are built and working today. Whether people actually return is unknown.

First customers

Where learners already ask for practice: the Intuit ProAdvisor community, r/Bookkeeping, course alumni groups. Later, course creators who want graded practice for their students.

Nothing is for sale yet. No revenue, users or retention exist; nothing on this slide is measured.8

What could kill it

Free is good enoughTest Drive plus YouTube may satisfy most learners. We have to show the check is worth paying for.
Nobody comes backThe daily file is our bet on a habit. If daily return stays low, the subscription case fails.
Realism ceilingGenerated files use four business types and seven problem kinds. Real cleanups are messier.
A shrinking fieldBLS projects bookkeeping clerk jobs down 6% from 2024 to 2034, still with about 170k openings a year.
An incumbentAkadian already sells practice to the same people, with an audience.
